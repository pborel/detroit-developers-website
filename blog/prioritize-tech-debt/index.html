// HOW TO GET YOUR TEAM TO PRIORITIZE TECH DEBT

How to get your team to prioritize tech debt

Apr 16, 2025 · Phil Borel · 3 min read

Note: this is an old post, ported from my old blog. I wrote it in April 2025 as the outline for a talk, memes & all.

1. Why is this a problem?

  • Trade-offs are hard.
  • Sprint points feel like a zero-sum game. Every point spent on tech debt is a point not spent on a feature someone is waiting for.
    • But it isn't actually zero-sum: less tech debt = greater efficiency in the future. The hard part is making that future efficiency visible to the people weighing it against a feature they can see today.

2. Understand your stakeholders' needs

Treat your PM, designer, EM & leadership the way you'd treat users: figure out what they need before you pitch them a solution. Start with a question: what does "tech debt" mean to the non-technical (or just other) stakeholders?

It's amorphous. It lacks a clear definition, or language to discuss trade-offs.

  • Solution: break tech debt down into "benefits" for an informed discussion of trade-offs. The conversation isn't "new feature or tech debt", it's "new feature or this specific performance improvement".

It has a reputation for being a black hole.

  • "It'll take 2 - 52 weeks to do this", or "it'll take 1 quarter" & then it takes 3 quarters.
  • It's typically worse before it's better, OR you have to overhaul a whole pattern across the entire codebase to get the benefit (a huge initial investment, or living with multiple patterns).
  • Solution: break down the problem (planning!) so you can give a realistic estimate & options (people love options).

3. Choose your path

1. 🌉 A culture that supports tech debt

  • The PM & EM trust engineering ICs to do the right thing. They don't need to know the ins & outs of the specific work & focus; they just trust you'll do the right thing.
  • This is really hard to achieve because you have to build up this trust by consistently doing the right thing in their eyes & for the business.
  • Popular for eng-run companies & small orgs that are extremely profitable / successful. It's easy to ask for budget when the money is flowing.

2. (Collaborative) non-compliance

  • It's not endorsed, but you can either just prioritize it & tell others to kick rocks, or
  • you can prioritize it without making it clear that you're working on it.
  • Popular for all orgs.

3. 🪣 Tech debt budgets

  • 20% of everyone's points per sprint, or
  • 1 sprint per quarter, or
  • 1 in 5 people focused 100% on tech debt
  • Popular for big orgs.

4. 💡 Redefine "tech debt" in a language the team can collaborate on

Ad hoc tech debt projects.

  • Tech debt is a huge category. Be very specific with your ask. Make each piece of tech debt its own project.
  • There's friction per instance, but you can now have an informed discussion of trade-offs.

5. ⭐ Break the false dichotomy of choice & mix 'n' match 1-4

You don't have to pick one. A budget gives you room to plan; specific, well-defined projects make the trade-offs discussable; delivering on both builds the trust that gets you closer to path 1.

4. Conclusion

  1. Empathize with your collaborators (PMs, Designers, other engineers).
  2. Build trust to help the cultural focus on tech debt & trade-offs improve.
    • DO NOT act in bad faith (non-compliance).
    • DO budget for tech debt. Start by using this time to define tech debt & plan how to solve it.
    • DO redefine tech debt: make trade-offs obvious.
      • Fair warning: this may result in not prioritizing some tech debt.
    • DO plan your tech debt removal so you can give accurate estimates.
